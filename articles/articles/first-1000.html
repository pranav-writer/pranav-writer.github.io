What Should You Do With Your First $1,000?

A Simple Order of Priorities

By Pranav Sharma
Finance & Personal Finance

Let’s start with this: if you suddenly have $1,000 available, should you invest it, save it, pay off debt, or just keep it as cash?

$1,000 feels like a lot of money when you’re starting out. Most of us just want to invest it quickly because, in the current market, you always feel like the market is running out of your hands. But remember, the best use of that money depends on your financial situation. In this article, I am stating what I would do in this situation.

First: Is There an Urgent Financial Problem?

Before investing, I want to be debt free, so I would like to pay my past-due bills. After that, if there is some debt that has a high interest rate, I would clear that and then put some money toward essential expenses.

Remember, there is little value in becoming a stock-market millionaire if you cannot cover your essential expenses. So avoid investing money you may need immediately to cover an emergency.

Second: Build a Starter Emergency Fund

An emergency fund is that amount which comes in handy when you face unexpected expenses, such as car repairs, medical bills, or sudden loss of income.

The primary function of an emergency fund is to be a financial cushion so that when something goes wrong, you don't have to use a credit card or go into debt.

I would also keep this money in an accessible place and not invest it in the stock market because you cannot plan for an emergency based on market conditions.

Start with $1,000, but also try to save enough to cover several months’ worth of expenses. What you’ll ultimately need depends on your monthly expenses, your job stability, and your personal situation. If you have a volatile income or higher essential expenses, you might need to build up a larger emergency cushion before you start putting extra money into investments.

Third: Look at High-Interest Debt

Credit-card debt is especially expensive because of high interest rates, and carrying a balance can make it hard to get ahead financially.

Paying down high-interest debt is one of the best ways to get a predictable financial benefit because you won’t have to pay that interest in the future.

But not all debt should be treated the same way. If your debt is low-interest, you may have more room to balance debt repayment with saving or investing.

Before you figure out what to do with your $1,000, take a look at the interest rate, monthly payment, and terms of your debt. If I were in that position, I would pay off expensive debt before investing long term.

Fourth: What About an Employer 401(k) Match?

If your employer has a 401(k) matching contribution, I would recommend taking advantage of that first before investing the rest of the money elsewhere.

An employer match is money that your company puts into your retirement account based on your contribution, according to the rules of the plan. Simply put, it can be a chance to receive additional retirement savings from your employer.

But the exact match will vary from employer to employer, so check your plan details before you commit.

Fifth: What If You Have No Expensive Debt and Already Have Emergency Savings?

If you have done all that, investing becomes much more reasonable.

Start investing and always diversify your portfolio. Remember, diversification is important in investing because no one can consistently predict short-term market movements.

What if you invest everything in a sector and then comes news that shakes all the companies in that sector? As they say, don't put all your eggs in the same basket.

Don't invest money you may need soon and don't chase returns simply because something is performing well.

The Conclusion

  • Urgent bills? → Handle them first.
  • High-interest debt? → Consider paying it down.
  • No emergency savings? → Build a starter emergency fund.
  • 401(k) match available? → Consider taking advantage according to plan rules.
  • Emergency fund + debt situation healthy? → Consider investing.
  • Need the money soon? → Keep it accessible rather than taking unnecessary market risk.
