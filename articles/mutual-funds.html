Why Mutual Funds Can Be a Practical Choice for Retail Investors

Finance & Personal Finance

By Pranav Sharma
Finance & Personal Finance

Introduction

Investing can seem complicated when you’re just getting started. You can choose from individual stocks, bonds, ETFs, mutual funds and other investment options. Mutual funds can be a more straightforward way for retail investors who lack the time or experience to research individual securities to build a diversified portfolio. Instead of picking each investment yourself, you can invest in a fund that has a basket of securities all based on a particular strategy. Mutual funds are not necessarily the best option for all investors and they come with fees, risks and different investment goals. Used properly, however, they can be a useful alternative for investors seeking diversification and professional management without having to manage each investment themselves.

Diversification

Mutual funds have one big advantage: diversification. A mutual fund can buy shares of several companies or other securities depending on the fund’s investment strategy, rather than putting your money into one company. This means that the performance of your investment is not dependent solely on one company.

A diversified stock mutual fund may reduce the impact of one company doing poorly in the fund's holdings because the fund holds many other investments as well. But diversification doesn’t insulate you from all risks, and a mutual fund can still lose value when the overall market declines.

This can be a boon for retail investors, as building a diversified portfolio yourself can be more research-intensive, time-consuming, and costly.

Professional Management

Mutual funds are also popular for professional management for retail investors. Rather than doing research and buying individual stocks or bonds, investors can select a fund that is managed with a specific investment approach.

The fund manager and their team research the investments, decide what to buy or sell and manage the portfolio within the stated objectives of the fund. This can be useful for investors who do not have the time, knowledge or interest to analyze individual securities.

Professional management does not, however, assure better returns. A fund may still not perform as well as you would like and actively managed funds may charge higher fees than some passive investment options. Investors should therefore consider a fund’s strategy, costs, risks and past performance before investing.

Retail Investor Accessibility

Mutual funds can also make investing easier for people who may not have a lot of money to invest. Rather than assembling a portfolio by buying a bunch of individual securities, an investor can buy shares in a mutual fund and in doing so gain exposure to the fund’s underlying investments.

This can make the process easier to understand and deal with for someone who is new to investing. Many mutual funds also have different investment objectives so that investors can pick funds based on what they are trying to accomplish.

But being accessible doesn’t mean that all mutual funds are right for all investors. Before investing, it is important to understand the investment strategy, fees, risks and minimum investment requirements of the fund.

Various Investment Objectives

Mutual funds are available for different investment goals and risk appetites. Some funds concentrate on stocks, others on bonds or a combination of different assets. This enables retail investors to choose a fund as per their financial objectives, investment time frame and risk tolerance.

For example, an investor seeking long-term growth may look at a stock mutual fund, while someone seeking income or lower volatility may look at a bond fund. There are also funds that mix different types of investments within a portfolio.

The key is to know what a fund owns before you buy it. A fund’s name doesn’t tell you whether it’s right for your particular situation.

The Cost Question

When investing in a mutual fund, it is important to consider the fees. Mutual funds have fees associated with managing and operating the fund and those costs can eat away at the return an investor ultimately receives.

Investors should be aware of the fund's expense ratio and understand what they are paying for. Two funds can have similar investment strategies but different costs. This can make a difference over a long investment period.

Lower fees don’t necessarily equate to a better fund and higher fees don’t necessarily guarantee better performance. What matters is to consider the cost in relation to the fund's investment approach, its level of risk and what sort of management it offers.

For retail investors, knowledge of these costs can help them be more informed about these different investment options.

Individual Stocks vs Mutual Funds

For the retail investor, one of the biggest distinctions between mutual funds and individual stocks is the level of control and responsibility. If you purchase one stock, your return depends heavily on how well that individual company performs. You also have to research the company and when to buy or sell.

With a mutual fund, your money is invested in a number of investments, according to the fund’s investment strategy. This can help reduce the concentration that comes with owning only a few individual stocks, as well as reduce the amount of individual security selection you have to do yourself.

It’s not a straightforward answer. Mutual funds can be useful for those who prefer a more diversified and professionally managed approach to investing. Individual stocks might be attractive for those who want more control and are comfortable doing their own research.

When Not to Invest in Mutual Funds

Mutual funds are a good idea for many retail investors but they are not a good idea for everyone. The investor should first look at their financial objectives, time horizon, risk tolerance and the costs of the fund.

If you want to select individual companies and run your own portfolio, you might want to buy individual stocks. And another investor might like an ETF for its trading flexibility or perhaps lower costs. Some mutual funds may have higher fees or investing restrictions that an investor needs to know before investing.

The point is, you don’t invest in a mutual fund just because it’s diversified or professionally managed. Investors need to know the fund’s holdings, how it is managed and how it helps in their own financial situation.

Conclusion

Mutual funds can be a practical investment choice for retail investors, offering diversification, professional management and access to different investment strategies through a single investment. But they’re not risk-free, and they’re not automatically better than individual stocks or ETFs. Investors should review a fund's strategy, costs, risks, and whether it matches their financial objectives before investing. If you want a diversified portfolio but you don’t want to pick and choose and manage every security, a mutual fund is one option to consider. It’s important to know what you’re investing in, not just to go with a fund that’s done well in the past.
